What is Individual Therapy?

Individual therapy, also called counseling or psychotherapy, is a collaborative process between you and a licensed mental health professional. In confidential one-on-one sessions, you'll work together to:

  • Understand the root causes of your struggles and emotional patterns
  • Develop healthy coping strategies for managing difficult emotions
  • Process traumatic experiences and painful memories in a safe environment
  • Set meaningful personal goals and create actionable plans to achieve them
  • Build resilience, self-awareness, and emotional regulation skills
  • Create lasting positive changes in your thoughts, feelings, and behaviors

Unlike talking to friends or family, therapy with a trained professional provides evidence-based techniques, an objective perspective free from personal bias, and a completely confidential space where you can be fully honest without fear of judgment.

Evidence-Based Therapy Approaches We Use

At Healing Therapy Center, our therapists are trained in multiple therapeutic modalities, allowing us to customize treatment to your unique needs and preferences:

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)

CBT is one of the most well-researched and effective therapy approaches for anxiety, depression, PTSD, OCD, and many other conditions. It focuses on identifying and changing unhelpful thought patterns and behaviors that contribute to your distress. Through CBT, you'll learn to recognize cognitive distortions, challenge negative automatic thoughts, test out new behaviors through homework assignments, and develop practical coping skills you can use daily.

CBT is particularly effective because it's structured, goal-oriented, and produces results relatively quickly compared to other approaches. Research shows that CBT creates lasting changes in brain chemistry and thought patterns.

Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT)

Originally developed for borderline personality disorder, DBT is now used for emotion regulation difficulties, self-harm behaviors, suicidal ideation, and intense emotional reactions. DBT teaches four core skill sets: mindfulness for staying present, distress tolerance for crisis situations, emotion regulation for managing intense feelings, and interpersonal effectiveness for healthy relationships.

EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing)

EMDR is a specialized therapy for trauma and PTSD that helps your brain process traumatic memories in a new way. Using bilateral stimulation (typically eye movements), EMDR allows you to reprocess disturbing memories so they no longer cause significant distress. Many clients experience relief from trauma symptoms more quickly with EMDR than with traditional talk therapy alone.

Mindfulness-Based Therapy

Mindfulness approaches teach you to observe your thoughts and feelings without judgment, stay present in the moment rather than ruminating about the past or worrying about the future, and respond to situations thoughtfully rather than react automatically. Mindfulness is particularly helpful for anxiety, depression, chronic pain, and stress-related conditions.

Psychodynamic & Insight-Oriented Therapy

This approach explores how past experiences, particularly early relationships, influence your current thoughts, feelings, and behavior patterns. Psychodynamic therapy helps you understand unconscious patterns, gain insight into recurring relationship dynamics, and work through unresolved conflicts from your past.

What to Expect in Individual Therapy at Our Dearborn Office

Your First Session

Your initial appointment is an opportunity for you and your therapist to get to know each other. You'll discuss what brought you to therapy, your goals and hopes for treatment, your personal history and current life situation, and any previous therapy or mental health treatment experiences. Your therapist will explain their approach, answer your questions about the therapy process, and develop an initial treatment plan together.

Typical Session Structure

Most individual therapy sessions last 50-60 minutes and occur weekly, though frequency can be adjusted based on your needs. A typical session might include checking in about your week and current concerns, working on specific issues or practicing new skills, processing emotions that arise during the session, and planning any homework or practice for the week ahead.

Duration of Treatment

The length of therapy varies greatly depending on your goals and needs. Some people benefit from short-term focused therapy (8-12 sessions) for specific issues, while others engage in longer-term therapy for deeper personal growth or complex trauma. Your therapist will regularly check in with you about progress and discuss when it might be appropriate to reduce frequency or conclude therapy.

Confidentiality

Everything you share in therapy is strictly confidential, with rare exceptions required by law (such as imminent danger to yourself or others, child or elder abuse, or court orders). Your therapist will explain confidentiality limits in detail during your first session so you understand exactly what's protected.

Who Can Benefit from Individual Therapy?

Individual therapy isn't just for people in crisis—it's for anyone who wants support with:

  • Managing mental health conditions like anxiety, depression, PTSD, or OCD
  • Navigating difficult life transitions such as divorce, loss, career changes, or major moves
  • Improving relationships by understanding patterns and developing communication skills
  • Processing past trauma that continues to affect your present
  • Developing coping skills for stress, anger, or overwhelming emotions
  • Personal growth and self-discovery when you feel stuck or unfulfilled
  • Making important life decisions with clarity and confidence
  • Breaking destructive patterns in relationships, work, or personal habits

You don't need to have a diagnosed mental illness or be in a crisis to benefit from therapy. Many of our clients in Dearborn and surrounding areas seek therapy for personal growth, self-improvement, or support during challenging times.

Frequently Asked Questions About Individual Therapy

How long does individual therapy take?

There's no set timeline for therapy—it depends on your goals, the complexity of your concerns, and how quickly you progress. Some people achieve their goals in 8-12 sessions (short-term therapy), while others benefit from longer-term support over several months or years. Many clients start with weekly sessions and gradually reduce frequency as they improve. Your therapist will regularly discuss progress and help you determine when you've met your goals.

Does insurance cover individual therapy in Michigan?

Most major insurance plans in Michigan cover individual therapy for mental health conditions. Coverage varies by plan, but typically includes a certain number of sessions per year with copays ranging from $10-$50 per session. We recommend calling your insurance provider or our office at (313) 654-1915 to verify your specific coverage, copay amounts, and deductible information before your first appointment.

What if I don't feel comfortable with my therapist?

The therapeutic relationship is the most important factor in successful therapy outcomes. It's completely normal and okay if you don't feel a good connection with your first therapist. We encourage you to discuss any concerns with your therapist first, as sometimes comfort builds over time. However, if after 2-3 sessions you still don't feel it's a good fit, we're happy to help you find a different therapist on our team whose style might be a better match for you. Your comfort and progress are our priorities.

Our self-pay rates for individual therapy sessions range from $100-$175 per 50-minute session, depending on the therapist's experience level and credentials. We offer a sliding scale fee structure for clients experiencing financial hardship, with rates as low as $75 per session based on income. We also provide superbills for clients with out-of-network insurance benefits who want to submit for reimbursement.

Can I do therapy online or do I have to come to your Dearborn office?

We offer both in-person therapy at our Dearborn location and secure telehealth (online video) therapy for Michigan residents. Research shows that telehealth therapy is equally effective as in-person therapy for most mental health concerns. Many of our clients appreciate the convenience of attending sessions from home, eliminating drive time and parking concerns. You can also switch between in-person and telehealth sessions based on your schedule and preferences.

How do I know if I need therapy?

Consider therapy if you're experiencing persistent feelings of sadness, anxiety, or hopelessness; struggling to manage daily responsibilities; using unhealthy coping mechanisms like substance use or self-harm; experiencing relationship conflicts or breakups; feeling stuck or unfulfilled in life; having intrusive thoughts or difficulty concentrating; or simply wanting professional support during a challenging time. You don't need to be in crisis to benefit from therapy—many people seek counseling for personal growth or to work through life transitions.

What's the difference between a therapist, psychologist, and psychiatrist?

These terms can be confusing! A therapist (or counselor) is a licensed mental health professional who provides talk therapy and can include Licensed Professional Counselors (LPC), Licensed Clinical Social Workers (LCSW), or Licensed Marriage and Family Therapists (LMFT). A psychologist has a doctoral degree (Ph.D. or Psy.D.) in psychology, provides therapy, and can conduct psychological testing. A psychiatrist is a medical doctor (M.D. or D.O.) who specializes in mental health and can prescribe medication, though they typically don't provide regular talk therapy. Our team includes various types of licensed therapists to meet your needs.

Will my therapist tell me what to do?

Good therapy is collaborative, not directive. While your therapist won't tell you exactly what to do (since only you know what's best for your life), they will help you explore your options, understand patterns in your thinking and behavior, challenge unhelpful beliefs, and develop skills to make decisions that align with your values and goals. Think of your therapist as a guide and partner in your journey, not someone who hands you a prescription for how to live.
