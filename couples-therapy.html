What is Couples Therapy?

Couples therapy (also called marriage counseling or relationship therapy) is a specialized form of psychotherapy where both partners meet with a trained therapist to improve their relationship. Unlike individual therapy, couples therapy focuses on the relationship itself—the patterns, communication styles, and emotional dynamics between partners.

During couples therapy sessions at our Dearborn office, you and your partner will work together with a licensed therapist to identify problem areas, develop healthier communication skills, and rebuild emotional intimacy. Our therapists create a safe, non-judgmental space where both partners feel heard and supported.

Common Relationship Issues We Help With in Dearborn

Every couple faces challenges. The difference between relationships that thrive and those that struggle often comes down to how partners address these challenges. Our couples therapists in Dearborn have extensive experience helping couples work through:

Communication Breakdown

Do your conversations always end in arguments? Do you feel like your partner doesn't listen or understand you? Communication problems are the most common reason couples seek therapy. We teach proven communication techniques like active listening, "I" statements, and emotional validation that help partners truly hear each other without defensiveness or criticism.

Constant Conflict & Arguments

If you're stuck in repetitive argument cycles about the same issues (money, parenting, housework, intimacy), couples therapy can help break these destructive patterns. We help you identify the underlying emotional needs driving the conflict and develop healthier ways to address disagreements without damaging your relationship.

Infidelity & Trust Issues

Recovering from an affair or betrayal is one of the most difficult challenges a relationship can face. Our therapists provide specialized support for couples healing from infidelity, helping both partners process the pain, rebuild trust gradually, and decide together whether the relationship can be repaired. Many couples emerge from this process with a stronger, more honest relationship than before.

Intimacy & Sexual Problems

Sexual intimacy issues affect many couples but are often difficult to discuss. Whether you're experiencing desire discrepancies, sexual dysfunction, intimacy avoidance, or emotional disconnection affecting your physical relationship, our therapists provide a safe, comfortable environment to address these sensitive topics and rebuild your intimate connection.

Parenting Disagreements

Disagreements about discipline, parenting styles, or co-parenting approaches can create significant tension between partners. We help couples align on parenting goals, respect different approaches, and present a united front to children while maintaining a strong partnership.

Life Transitions & Major Changes

Major life changes—marriage, new baby, job loss, relocation, empty nest, retirement—can strain even strong relationships. Couples therapy helps partners navigate these transitions together, supporting each other through change rather than letting stress drive you apart.

Emotional Disconnection & Distance

Do you feel like roommates rather than romantic partners? Has the emotional spark faded? Many couples describe feeling lonely even when together. We help partners reconnect emotionally, rediscover what brought them together, and rebuild the friendship and romance that may have been lost to busy schedules and daily stress.

Blended Family Challenges

Navigating stepfamily dynamics, ex-partner relationships, and co-parenting across households creates unique challenges. Our therapists help blended families establish healthy boundaries, manage loyalty conflicts, and build cohesive family units that honor all relationships involved.

Cultural & Religious Differences

Partners from different cultural, religious, or family backgrounds may face unique challenges around traditions, values, and expectations. We help couples honor both backgrounds while creating shared values and traditions that strengthen your unique partnership.

Financial Stress & Money Conflicts

Money disagreements are a leading source of relationship stress. Whether you have different spending styles, income disparities, debt concerns, or conflicting financial goals, we help couples develop healthy financial communication and aligned money management strategies.

Evidence-Based Couples Therapy Approaches We Use

At Healing Therapy Center in Dearborn, our marriage counselors are trained in multiple therapeutic approaches and tailor treatment to each couple's unique needs. Our primary methods include:

Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT)

EFT is one of the most researched and effective approaches to couples therapy, with over 70% of couples showing significant improvement. EFT focuses on identifying and transforming the negative emotional patterns that create distance and conflict. By understanding the deeper attachment needs driving your reactions, you and your partner learn to create more secure emotional bonds and respond to each other with compassion rather than defensiveness. EFT is particularly effective for couples struggling with emotional disconnection, recurring conflicts, and trust issues.

The Gottman Method

Based on 40+ years of research by Drs. John and Julie Gottman, this approach focuses on building friendship, managing conflict constructively, and creating shared meaning in relationships. The Gottman Method helps couples strengthen their "friendship system," learn to manage perpetual problems (issues that never fully resolve), and avoid the "Four Horsemen" of relationship apocalypse: criticism, contempt, defensiveness, and stonewalling. This approach is highly practical, teaching specific skills you can use immediately.

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) for Couples

CBT helps couples identify thought patterns and behaviors that damage the relationship and replace them with healthier alternatives. If you find yourselves making negative assumptions about each other's intentions or engaging in destructive behavior patterns, CBT provides practical tools to change these patterns and create more positive interactions.

Imago Relationship Therapy

This approach helps couples understand how childhood experiences and unconscious expectations shape current relationship dynamics. By understanding why certain issues trigger intense reactions, couples develop greater empathy and compassion for each other's vulnerabilities and learn to heal old wounds within the safety of their relationship.

Solution-Focused Brief Therapy

For couples dealing with specific, concrete issues, solution-focused therapy emphasizes your strengths and resources, helping you envision your ideal relationship and take practical steps to create it. This approach is particularly effective for couples who want focused, goal-oriented work on specific problems.

When Should You Seek Couples Therapy?

Many couples wait too long before seeking help—an average of six years after problems begin. Early intervention leads to better outcomes. Consider couples therapy if:

  • You argue about the same issues repeatedly without resolution
  • Communication has become negative, defensive, or has broken down entirely
  • One or both partners feel emotionally distant or disconnected
  • You're considering separation or divorce but aren't sure
  • Infidelity or betrayal has damaged trust
  • Intimacy (emotional or physical) has significantly decreased
  • Life changes have created stress in your relationship
  • You want to strengthen your relationship before problems escalate (premarital counseling)
  • Past trauma or mental health issues are affecting your relationship
  • You feel more like roommates than romantic partners
  • Your partner has suggested therapy (even if you're hesitant)

You don't have to be in crisis to benefit from couples therapy. Many couples use therapy as "relationship maintenance"—working with a therapist periodically to strengthen their bond and address small issues before they become serious problems.

What to Expect in Couples Therapy Sessions at Our Dearborn Office

First Session: Comprehensive Assessment

Your first couples therapy session (typically 60-90 minutes) involves both partners meeting together with your therapist. We'll discuss your relationship history, what brought you to therapy, your goals for counseling, and current challenges. Your therapist will observe how you communicate and interact. Some therapists may also schedule brief individual sessions to understand each partner's perspective privately.

Ongoing Sessions: Building Skills & Processing Emotions

Couples therapy sessions are typically weekly or biweekly, lasting 50-60 minutes. Sessions focus on improving communication patterns, processing difficult emotions in a safe environment, learning conflict resolution skills, and rebuilding emotional connection. Your therapist may assign "homework" between sessions to practice new skills and strengthen progress.

Active Participation from Both Partners

Successful couples therapy requires commitment from both partners. While it's normal for one partner to be more enthusiastic about therapy initially, both partners need to engage honestly and work toward change. Your therapist remains neutral, supporting both partners equally without taking sides.

Timeline & What Success Looks Like

Most couples attend therapy for 8-20 sessions, though this varies greatly depending on the severity of issues and your goals. Some couples see improvement within a few sessions; others benefit from longer-term work. Success doesn't mean never arguing—it means having the tools to navigate conflict constructively, feeling emotionally connected, and being able to repair ruptures when they occur.

Does Couples Therapy Work? Success Rates & Research

Research shows that couples therapy is highly effective when both partners are committed to the process:

  • 70% of couples report significant relationship improvement after couples therapy (American Association for Marriage and Family Therapy)
  • Emotionally Focused Therapy shows 70-75% of couples moving from distress to recovery, with 90% showing significant improvement
  • The Gottman Method demonstrates significant positive effects in relationship quality and communication
  • Couples who seek therapy earlier in their relationship problems have better outcomes than those who wait years

That said, couples therapy doesn't "save" every relationship—and that's sometimes the right outcome. For some couples, therapy helps them reach clarity about whether to stay together or provides tools for a healthier separation and co-parenting relationship if needed.

Premarital Counseling: Starting Your Marriage Strong

Why wait for problems to develop? Premarital counseling helps engaged couples build a strong foundation before marriage by:

  • Discussing expectations about marriage, family, career, and lifestyle
  • Learning healthy communication and conflict resolution skills
  • Addressing potential areas of conflict before they become problems
  • Exploring values around finances, parenting, intimacy, and family relationships
  • Building a strong friendship and emotional connection
  • Understanding each other's family backgrounds and how they influence expectations

Research shows that couples who complete premarital counseling have a 30% higher marital success rate than those who don't.

Frequently Asked Questions About Couples Therapy

Does insurance cover couples therapy in Michigan?

Many insurance plans do cover couples therapy, especially when one partner has a diagnosable mental health condition (such as anxiety or depression) that affects the relationship. Coverage varies by plan. We recommend calling your insurance provider to verify benefits, and our office can help you understand your coverage when you call (313) 654-1915.

What if my partner doesn't want to go to couples therapy?

It's common for one partner to be more hesitant about therapy initially. If your partner is resistant, consider individual therapy first to work on your own communication and relationship patterns—this often creates positive changes that can motivate a hesitant partner to participate. You can also suggest a "trial" of just a few sessions to see if it's helpful. Many initially reluctant partners become engaged once they experience therapy in a safe, non-judgmental environment.

Will the therapist take sides or tell us to break up?

Ethical couples therapists remain neutral and work for the relationship system, not for either individual partner. We don't tell couples to stay together or break up—that decision is always yours. Our role is to help you communicate more effectively, understand each other better, and make informed decisions about your relationship from a place of clarity rather than reactivity.

How long does couples therapy take?

This varies significantly based on the severity of issues, how long problems have existed, and both partners' commitment to change. Some couples see improvement in 8-12 sessions for focused issues, while couples dealing with infidelity, severe communication breakdown, or long-standing patterns may benefit from 20+ sessions. Your therapist will work with you to set goals and assess progress regularly.

Can couples therapy help if we're already considering divorce?

Yes. Couples therapy can help even when divorce seems likely. Sometimes therapy helps couples reconnect and rebuild their relationship. Other times, it helps couples gain clarity about the decision to separate and provides tools for a healthier, less contentious divorce and co-parenting relationship. Either outcome is valuable—what matters is that you make the decision from a place of clarity and effort rather than regret.

What if we've tried couples therapy before and it didn't work?

Not all therapists are equally skilled in couples work, and the fit between couple and therapist matters greatly. If previous therapy wasn't helpful, consider whether the therapist was specifically trained in couples therapy, whether both partners were equally committed, and whether you gave the process enough time. A different therapist with specialized training and a different approach may lead to very different results.

Is couples therapy just venting to a therapist, or will we learn actual skills?

While processing emotions is part of couples therapy, evidence-based approaches teach concrete, practical skills you can use immediately: active listening techniques, conflict de-escalation strategies, emotional regulation tools, and ways to rebuild intimacy and friendship. You'll practice these skills in session and apply them at home between sessions.

How much does couples therapy cost in Dearborn?

Our couples therapy rates are competitive with the Metro Detroit area. Session costs vary depending on whether you use insurance (copay/coinsurance rates) or self-pay. Many couples find that investing in their relationship through therapy is far less expensive—financially and emotionally—than separation or divorce. Call us at (313) 654-1915 for specific pricing and to verify your insurance coverage.
